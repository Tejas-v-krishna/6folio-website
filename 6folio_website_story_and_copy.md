# 1. The central story

## The chosen idea: Six perspectives. One clear direction.

6FOLIO is built around a simple truth: a good brand rarely grows from one kind of thinking alone.

A business needs someone to understand what makes it different. It needs someone to find the right words. It needs images that make people look twice. It needs film that gives an idea movement. It needs a plan to reach the right audience. It needs a digital experience that turns interest into action.

Those are different ways of seeing the same problem. At 6FOLIO, six collaborators bring those perspectives to one table and move in the same direction.

The story is not that six people can do six separate jobs. The story is that six points of view can create one joined-up piece of work when they share the same brief, the same purpose, and the same standard.

**Core brand thought:** Different disciplines. Shared direction. Work that connects.

**Primary tagline:** Six perspectives. One clear direction.

**Supporting line:** Six people, working across strategy, content, production, marketing, and digital experience.

**Short brand description:** 6FOLIO is an independent creative growth studio bringing six complementary perspectives together to help ambitious businesses build a clear identity, communicate with purpose, and grow across digital channels.

## The story in one paragraph

Every business has a story. Getting it understood takes more than a logo, a few posts, or a new website. It takes a clear point of view, the right words, thoughtful visuals, useful distribution, and a digital experience built around what people need next. 6FOLIO brings six collaborators into that process. Each sees the work from a different angle. Together, we make the parts work as one.

## The story in one sentence

Six people bring different perspectives to one shared goal: making good businesses easier to understand, remember, and choose.

## The emotional idea

The visitor should feel that 6FOLIO is thoughtful, capable, collaborative, and creatively ambitious. The work should feel considered rather than loud. The team should feel close enough to care about the details and experienced enough to understand the business behind the brief.

## What the story must not become

- Do not invent a dramatic origin story, childhood friendship, late-night studio moment, or claim that the six founders have worked together for years unless it is factually true.
- Do not imply that six people are six departments or that every person is an expert in every discipline.
- Do not make the number six feel like a gimmick. It should explain the way the team thinks and works.
- Do not say that 6FOLIO guarantees sales, virality, rankings, leads, or a specific return on ad spend.
- Do not use vague claims such as "we do it all", "we make magic", or "we take brands to the next level".

# 10. Art direction: premium, specific, and deliberately not AI-slop

## Creative direction

The site should feel like an independent creative studio with a strong editorial eye. Think art direction, typography, composition, image sequencing, and precise interactions. It should not look like a prebuilt SaaS landing page with an agency logo pasted on top.

**Visual keywords:** editorial, graphic, tactile, confident, art-directed, asymmetric, precise, human, restrained, contemporary.

**The design principle:** Let the work and the typography carry the identity. Use interaction to reveal thought, not to decorate empty space.

## Recommended visual system

### Colour
Start with a restrained palette:
- Warm paper or soft bone as the main light surface.
- Near-black or ink for primary text and dark sections.
- One strong signature colour chosen after testing the wordmark and portfolio imagery.
- A muted neutral for secondary text, rules, and metadata.

Do not add several accent colours just to make the site feel energetic. The work itself should provide colour variation.

### Typography
Use a deliberate two-typeface system:
- A distinctive editorial display face for headlines.
- A highly legible sans-serif for body copy, navigation, metadata, and forms.

Use fluid type sizing with a clear scale. Avoid using too many font weights or mixing several unrelated typefaces. Test long headlines, short labels, and paragraph reading on real devices.

### Layout
- Use a clear grid, but allow selected sections to break the grid with purpose.
- Give the typography room to breathe.
- Use generous but controlled vertical rhythm.
- Let images vary in scale and crop, while maintaining a coherent image language.
- Use thin rules, labels, issue-like numbering, and restrained metadata to create editorial structure.
- Make the six-part system visible through numbering and sequencing rather than six identical decorative cards.

### Image direction
- Prioritise original photography, properly licensed images, or bespoke visual work.
- Show real products, real environments, real process, and details that support each project story.
- Use consistent grading within each project, not one artificial filter over the entire site.
- Use purposeful crops and compositions. A strong image should communicate something about the work.
- For concept projects, produce original assets specifically for the concept rather than using unrelated stock images.

### Motion direction
- Use motion to support hierarchy and reveal content.
- Prefer well-timed transitions, image reveals, typography movement, subtle parallax, and meaningful hover states.
- Keep navigation and reading predictable.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep animation smooth on mid-range phones and laptops.
- Never make essential content available only through animation.

## Do not use these AI-slop design patterns

- Purple-blue-pink gradient backgrounds with glowing blobs.
- Generic glassmorphism panels floating over a gradient.
- Random 3D spheres, chrome objects, liquid shapes, or abstract blobs without a brand reason.
- A giant rounded hero card with a generic headline and three feature cards underneath.
- Six identical rounded service cards with random Lucide icons as the main visual idea.
- Fake dashboards, fake analytics screenshots, or meaningless statistics.
- Stock photos of anonymous teams high-fiving around a laptop.
- Overused AI-generated abstract human figures or impossible product mockups.
- Excessive pill buttons, giant radii, drop shadows, and identical card treatments on every section.
- Fake awards, partner logos, client logos, press mentions, testimonials, or trust badges.
- A cursor-following effect that makes the site harder to use.
- Scroll hijacking, forced horizontal scrolling, or animations that delay access to content.
- Copy such as "we turn dreams into reality", "where innovation meets creativity", "unlock your potential", "redefine the future", or "we make magic happen".

## Interaction details worth exploring

Choose only the interactions that strengthen the story:

1. **Six-perspective index:** As the visitor scrolls through the six disciplines, a persistent index or progress marker moves from 01 to 06. It should remain subtle and usable.
2. **Work as editorial spreads:** Project cards can open into immersive case-study layouts with deliberate image sequencing and clear project metadata.
3. **A shared-direction graphic:** A simple typographic or geometric composition can show six independent lines converging into one direction. Keep it custom and minimal, not a generic animated logo reveal.
4. **Image-led transitions:** Images can reveal through clean masks or restrained crops. Avoid heavy effects that hurt performance.
5. **Useful hover feedback:** Show the project category, title, or a small directional cue. Do not animate every element on the screen.

# 12. Design and development quality bar

The first version can be limited in scope, but it must not feel careless or unfinished.

## Required quality

- Responsive from small mobile screens through wide desktop screens.
- Strong typography and clear reading hierarchy.
- Accessible colour contrast and visible keyboard focus.
- Semantic HTML and meaningful heading order.
- Useful alt text for informative images and empty alt text for decorative images.
- Functional navigation and working project links.
- Functional contact form or an explicit prototype state.
- Optimised image formats and responsive image sizing.
- No horizontal overflow at common viewport sizes.
- No console errors or broken assets.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Sensible SEO title, meta description, Open Graph image, and favicon.
- Fast loading and minimal unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
- No fake content that could be mistaken for real clients or results.

## Suggested technical approach

Use the existing repository's framework and conventions if a project already exists. Do not rewrite the stack just because a different framework is fashionable.

If starting from an empty repository, a reasonable default is:
- Next.js with TypeScript.
- Tailwind CSS or a small, well-organised CSS system.
- A reusable component structure.
- A lightweight motion library only where it adds real value.
- Local project data for case studies in the first version.
- A contact form connected to a real endpoint only when credentials and a destination are configured.

Do not install a large library for a small effect. Keep components understandable and easy to edit.

# 13. Overnight autopilot instructions for the AI coding agent

## Mission

Build the first complete, responsive 6FOLIO website using this document as the source of truth for story, content, layout, and art direction. The goal is a coherent first release, not an endless design exploration.

## Operating rules

1. Inspect the repository before changing anything. Identify the framework, package manager, existing routes, components, assets, and current state.
2. Preserve working code and existing project conventions. Do not delete or replace files without understanding their purpose.
3. Read this document fully before implementation. Treat the written brand story and copy as approved starting content.
4. If the repository already contains a design system, adapt it rather than creating a competing one.
5. If the repository is empty, choose a stable, familiar stack and explain the choice in the final handover.
6. Implement the full first-release page structure. Do not stop after creating only a hero section.
7. Use real assets already present in the repository where appropriate. If no assets exist, create a consistent visual placeholder system using local, abstract typography and layout treatments. Do not fetch random stock images or pretend placeholders are real work.
8. If using temporary images, make their placeholder status obvious in the code and document exactly which assets need replacing.
9. Use the supplied copy. Do not rewrite it into generic AI marketing language. Do not add em dashes.
10. Label all self-initiated projects as concept work. Never invent client outcomes, testimonials, awards, clients, partners, team names, or contact details.
11. Build the mobile layout as carefully as the desktop layout. Do not treat mobile as a compressed version of desktop.
12. Keep motion restrained, purposeful, performant, and accessible. Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.
13. Make all visible controls work. A button must navigate, submit, or have a clear action. Remove any control that has no function.
14. Do not create a non-functional contact form that claims an enquiry was sent. Use a clear prototype state until a real submission endpoint is configured.
15. Do not add analytics, trackers, external services, paid APIs, or services requiring credentials unless they are already configured and authorised.
16. Do not expose API keys or secrets in client-side code. Do not invent credentials.
17. Avoid needless dependencies. Do not add heavy animation or UI libraries for effects that can be implemented simply.
18. Run the available lint, typecheck, and build commands. Fix the errors that are introduced by the work.
19. Check the site at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths using available tools. Fix overflow, broken spacing, clipping, and navigation issues.
20. If an action requires a missing credential, real business detail, team portrait, or human approval, leave a clear placeholder and continue with the rest of the build.
