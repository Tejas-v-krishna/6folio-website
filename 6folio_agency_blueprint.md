**Document type:** Brand and digital agency project foundation  
**Working brand:** 6FOLIO  
**Working tagline:** Six disciplines. One growth system.  
**Prepared:** 31 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish the agency from zero and give an AI coding agent a detailed brief for building its first website.

# 0. Executive direction

6FOLIO should be positioned as an **integrated creative growth studio** rather than a generic digital marketing agency.

The agency brings together six connected capabilities: brand strategy, content strategy, visual production, social media, performance marketing, and web/digital growth. The value is not simply that clients can buy six services from one company. The value is that all six disciplines work from the same business objective, audience insight, creative direction, and measurement plan.

**Core positioning statement**

> 6FOLIO is an integrated creative and digital growth studio helping ambitious businesses build a distinctive brand, create meaningful content, reach the right audience, and turn attention into measurable action.

**Primary promise**

> One connected team from first idea to measurable growth.

**Brand tagline**

> Six disciplines. One growth system.

**The feeling the brand should create:** confident, thoughtful, contemporary, visually distinctive, commercially aware, and easy to work with.

Do not position 6FOLIO as the agency that does absolutely everything for absolutely everyone. Start with a clear core offer, demonstrate how the disciplines connect, and expand the offer only when the team can deliver the work consistently.

## The main strategic difference

A typical agency presents a list of services. 6FOLIO should present a connected journey:

**Understand → Position → Create → Distribute → Convert → Optimise**

Every project should begin with a business problem and end with a review of what changed. A beautiful campaign is not automatically a successful campaign. The work should be judged against the goal agreed with the client.

# 1. The six service pillars

Keep six primary service pillars visible in the brand and website. Individual deliverables can sit beneath each pillar.

## 01 — Brand Strategy & Identity

**Purpose:** Make a business recognisable, coherent, and relevant to the audience it wants to reach.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Brand discovery and competitor review.
- Audience and positioning workshops.
- Brand purpose, value proposition, and differentiation.
- Naming and messaging support where required.
- Visual identity direction, logo system, typography, colour, and art direction.
- Brand guidelines and rollout assets.

**Best suited to:** New businesses, startups preparing to launch, and existing brands that have outgrown their original identity.

## 02 — Content Strategy & Copywriting

**Purpose:** Decide what the brand should say, why it matters, and how it should communicate consistently.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Audience and channel research.
- Content pillars and editorial strategy.
- Campaign concepts and content calendars.
- Brand voice, captions, scripts, landing-page copy, and messaging.
- Content briefs for photography and video production.
- Thought-leadership and founder-content planning.

**Best suited to:** Businesses that post inconsistently, struggle to explain their value, or need a repeatable content system.

## 03 — Photography, Video & Motion Production

**Purpose:** Give the brand distinctive visual material that can be used across its website, advertising, social channels, and campaigns.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Product and e-commerce photography.
- Lifestyle, founder, team, and location shoots.
- Commercial films and campaign videos.
- Short-form vertical video and reels.
- Editing, colour grading, motion graphics, and cut-downs.
- Creative direction, shot lists, and production planning.

**Best suited to:** D2C, fashion, lifestyle, hospitality, food, consumer products, and service businesses that need strong visual communication.

## 04 — Social Media & Community

**Purpose:** Build a consistent and useful presence where the audience already spends time.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Platform selection and channel strategy.
- Monthly content calendars.
- Content adaptation and publishing.
- Community engagement and moderation.
- Social listening and audience feedback.
- Monthly channel reports and recommendations.

**Best suited to:** Brands that need a reliable publishing system and meaningful audience relationships, not just a higher post count.

## 05 — Performance Marketing & Lead Generation

**Purpose:** Use paid distribution to reach relevant audiences and generate qualified actions.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Paid media strategy and campaign planning.
- Meta Ads, Google Ads, and LinkedIn Ads where relevant.
- Audience research and campaign setup.
- Creative testing and landing-page coordination.
- Lead-generation funnels and retargeting.
- Budget pacing, optimisation, and transparent reporting.

**Best suited to:** Businesses with a clear offer, a defined audience, and the capacity to respond to leads or fulfil orders.

**Important:** Never promise guaranteed leads, sales, or ROAS. Explain that performance depends on the offer, market, budget, website, sales process, and other factors.

## 06 — Web & Digital Growth

**Purpose:** Turn the brand's digital presence into a useful experience that supports discovery, trust, and conversion.

**Services and deliverables:**
- Brand websites and landing pages.
- E-commerce and campaign websites.
- UX structure, interface design, and development.
- Technical and on-page SEO foundations.
- Conversion-rate optimisation and usability improvements.
- Analytics setup, event tracking, and reporting.
- CRM, email, and workflow automation where appropriate.

**Best suited to:** Startups that need a credible launch presence, businesses with outdated websites, and brands that want a better path from visitor to enquiry or purchase.

- Influencer and creator partnerships.
- Email and SMS lifecycle marketing.

## Roles to add as the agency grows

1. **Creative Director / Brand Designer** — owns the overall creative standard, visual identity, campaign art direction, and design consistency. This role becomes especially important when several designers and production specialists work together.
2. **Social Media & Community Manager** — manages publishing, platform operations, engagement, community moderation, and reporting. This should not be an invisible extra responsibility placed on the content strategist.
3. **SEO & Analytics Specialist** — owns search strategy, technical audits, keyword research, measurement plans, and reporting quality. Can begin as a trusted freelancer or specialist partner.
4. **Account Manager / Client Success Lead** — becomes useful when the number of active clients makes it difficult for the Delivery Producer to give every client enough attention.
5. **Designer / Motion Designer** — supports campaign design, social assets, brand systems, and motion work as demand increases.
6. **Creator Partnerships / Influencer Manager** — develops and manages creator relationships when this becomes a real revenue stream.

## Team design principles

- Do not hire every role full-time immediately. Use a compact core team with trusted specialist freelancers where demand is irregular.
- Every client must have **one named point of contact**.
- Every deliverable must have **one accountable owner** and a clear approval process.
- The client partner / delivery producer owns the schedule; specialists own the quality of their discipline.
- The creative director or designated creative owner signs off on visual consistency.
- The growth lead defines success metrics before campaigns begin.
- The developer owns technical quality, accessibility, performance, and secure integrations.
- Keep a written scope. New requests should be assessed for cost, time, and impact before being accepted.

# 3. Ideal clients and market entry

Do not launch with the message "we work with every type of business." A broad capability can be useful, but a broad message makes it harder for a new agency to be remembered.

## Recommended initial audience

Start with two or three segments where the team can build strong visual and digital work:

- **Consumer and D2C brands:** fashion, beauty, food, lifestyle, home, and personal products.
- **Early-stage startups:** technology, SaaS, consumer apps, and founder-led businesses preparing to launch or grow.
- **Experience-led businesses:** hospitality, fitness, wellness, and premium local services.

These are starting hypotheses, not permanent limits. Validate them through real client conversations and the team's ability to produce credible work.

## Ideal client signals

- Has a real product or service and a clear business objective.
- Values quality and is willing to collaborate.
- Can provide timely approvals and access to required information.
- Has a realistic budget for the scope.
- Is ready to measure outcomes, not only visual output.

## Avoid at the beginning

- Clients expecting guaranteed sales or viral content.
- Clients with no clear offer, no fulfilment capacity, or no willingness to collaborate.
- Unlimited revisions without a defined process.
- Very broad work scopes with tiny budgets.
- Campaigns where tracking, permissions, or advertising account access cannot be arranged.

# 4. The 6FOLIO delivery method

Give the agency a repeatable process that becomes part of the story on the website and in client proposals.

## The 6FOLIO Growth Loop

### 1. Understand

Discover the business, offer, audience, competitors, current challenges, and desired outcome. Collect available analytics and existing brand assets.

**Output:** discovery summary, problem definition, audience assumptions, and baseline measures.

### 2. Position

Decide what the brand should stand for, who it should reach, what message should lead, and which channels deserve attention.

**Output:** positioning direction, key message, creative direction, and priority channel plan.

### 3. Create

Develop the identity, content plan, scripts, visuals, website experience, and campaign assets needed for the agreed goal.

**Output:** approved creative assets, copy, website or landing-page components, and campaign materials.

### 4. Distribute

Publish content, launch campaigns, activate channels, and make sure the audience can take the next step.

**Output:** live content, campaigns, landing pages, and tracking checks.

### 5. Convert

Improve the path from attention to enquiry, booking, sign-up, or purchase. Fix friction in the message, landing page, form, or follow-up process.

**Output:** tested conversion journey and a record of changes made.

### 6. Optimise

Review performance, learn from feedback, identify what worked, and agree the next set of experiments.

**Output:** transparent report, insights, next-month priorities, and updated test plan.

## Engagement formats

Offer two simple ways to work together:

**Project engagements:** defined work such as a brand launch, campaign production, website, or product launch. These have a written scope, milestones, review rounds, and handover.

**Ongoing growth partnerships:** an agreed monthly scope across selected service pillars. These include a planning session, monthly priorities, regular check-ins, and a performance review. Never imply that every client needs all six services every month.

## Example first 90 days

| Period | Focus | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–15 | Discovery and direction | Business review, audience, positioning, analytics baseline, priorities, scope. |
| Days 16–30 | Build the foundation | Brand/content direction, creative production plan, website or landing-page plan, measurement setup. |
| Days 31–60 | Launch and distribute | Publish content, launch website/campaigns, test creative and channel assumptions. |
| Days 61–90 | Learn and improve | Review results, improve weak points, scale what is working responsibly, agree next-quarter plan. |

This is an example planning model, not a promise that every project can be completed in 90 days.

# 5. Brand and messaging direction

## Brand character

- **Confident:** speak clearly and avoid exaggerated claims.
- **Curious:** ask better questions before jumping into execution.
- **Inventive:** show original thinking and thoughtful craft.
- **Connected:** communicate that strategy, content, technology, and growth work together.
- **Accountable:** make scope, responsibilities, and performance visible.

## Messaging options

**Primary tagline:** Six disciplines. One growth system.

Alternative lines to test:
- One team. Every touchpoint connected.
- From first impression to lasting growth.
- Strategy, creativity, and technology in sync.
- Build the brand. Create the momentum.

Use one lead line consistently. Avoid rotating taglines across every section.

## Visual identity direction

The existing 6F monogram concept can become the visual foundation. Keep the symbol legible at small sizes and do not force every page element into the logo's geometry.

Recommended direction:
- Premium editorial layout with strong typography and generous spacing.
- A mostly monochrome base: near-black, warm white, and controlled grey values.
- One bright accent colour, such as electric cobalt or acid lime, used selectively for interaction and emphasis. Test it for accessibility before finalising.
- Real photography and film stills should provide much of the colour and personality.
- Use the number six as a subtle structural device: six service disciplines, six growth stages, six modular visual cells, or a restrained six-part graphic system.
- Use a clear grid and deliberate asymmetry around the symmetrical 6F mark.
- Avoid generic gradient blobs, random floating 3D objects, and decorative motion that has no relationship to the brand.

## Motion language

- A short, elegant 6F mark reveal.
- Typography that responds subtly to scroll or hover.
- Project imagery with deliberate transitions.
- A six-part progress or chapter system for the Growth Loop.
- A restrained cursor interaction only where it adds useful feedback.
- Motion should respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and remain understandable without animation.
- Sound must be off by default. Do not force the visitor to wait through a long intro.

# 6. Website strategy and information architecture

The website must do two jobs at once:

1. Make people remember 6FOLIO.
2. Make a potential client understand the offer, trust the team, and take the next step.

Awards-level craft is a design ambition, not a substitute for usability or conversion. Prioritise a distinctive idea, excellent typography, original content, real proof, speed, and a clear contact path.

## Primary navigation

- Work
- Services
- Approach
- About
- Contact

Keep the navigation short. Use a consistent contact CTA such as **Start a Project**.

## Homepage section sequence

### 1. Opening / Hero

A clear statement of what 6FOLIO does, a visual expression of the 6F identity, and one clear CTA.

Suggested headline:

> We build brands people notice, content people remember, and digital experiences that move business forward.

Supporting line:

> Strategy, content, production, marketing, and technology working as one connected team.

CTA: **Start a Project**  
Secondary link: **Explore Our Work**

Do not fill the hero with a paragraph of service keywords. The first screen should establish the point of view quickly.

### 2. Selected work

Show three to six strongest projects. Each card should explain the project in a few words and identify the relevant service or industry.

Use real images or original concept work. If a project is self-initiated, label it clearly as **Concept Project** or **Self-Initiated Work**. Do not imply it was commissioned by a real client.

### 3. The six disciplines

Show the six service pillars as a connected system. Every service should have a one-sentence outcome, a few deliverables, and a route to the relevant detail page or section.

### 4. The 6FOLIO Growth Loop

Explain Understand → Position → Create → Distribute → Convert → Optimise. Use a simple visual sequence that can be understood without interacting with it.

### 5. Why 6FOLIO

Explain the agency's way of working: a shared strategy, one point of contact, connected creative and technical delivery, transparent reporting, and a defined process.

Do not claim "better than every competitor". Demonstrate the difference through the process and the work.

### 6. About / Team

Introduce the team by role and capability. Use actual team portraits when available. Do not invent names, biographies, credentials, or headshots.

### 7. A useful point of view

Optional short insights or a behind-the-scenes section. Publish only when there is something genuinely useful to say. Do not create empty blog cards to make the site look busy.

### 8. Contact / Project enquiry

Make the final action obvious. The form should ask only for information needed to start a conversation:
- Name and work email.
- Company / brand.
- Website or social link (optional).
- What the business needs help with.
- Approximate budget range (optional but useful).
- Desired timeline.
- A short description of the goal.

Show a clear privacy note and a real contact email once one is confirmed.

## Essential supporting pages

- **Work index:** filterable projects by service and/or industry.
- **Case-study detail:** challenge, insight, strategy, execution, deliverables, and verified outcome.
- **Services:** detail pages or structured sections for each of the six pillars.
- **About:** agency philosophy, team, and ways of working.
- **Contact:** project enquiry and confirmed contact details.
- **Privacy policy:** publish an appropriate policy before collecting personal information.

## Case-study template

Every real case study should follow this structure:

1. **Project snapshot:** client, industry, services, timeline, and team.
2. **The challenge:** what was not working or what opportunity existed.
3. **The insight:** what research, observation, or business context shaped the approach.
4. **The strategy:** the decisions made and why.
5. **The execution:** identity, content, production, campaigns, website, or other deliverables.
6. **The outcome:** only verified metrics, feedback, or observable deliverables.
7. **The learning:** what the team would carry into the next phase.

Never invent clients, testimonials, revenue growth, leads, conversion rates, impressions, awards, or campaign results. Where the agency has no client work yet, use well-crafted self-initiated concepts and label them honestly.

# 8. Art direction and design system requirements

## Design principles

- Distinctive, editorial, and premium; not an off-the-shelf SaaS template.
- Work-first: original project visuals should carry the visual identity.
- Strong typography and clear content hierarchy.
- A repeatable spacing and grid system.
- Colour used with intention, not as decoration.
- Interaction that explains, guides, or rewards exploration.
- Every key message and CTA must remain understandable on mobile.

## Suggested visual ingredients

- 6F monogram as the core symbol.
- Six-cell or six-chapter graphic motif used sparingly.
- Oversized display typography balanced with readable body text.
- Large editorial project imagery.
- Short labels, project metadata, and deliberate section transitions.
- A clear, consistent button and link system.

## Responsive requirements

Design and test at minimum:
- Small mobile: 360–390 px wide.
- Large mobile: 430 px wide.
- Tablet: around 768 px wide.
- Laptop: around 1440 px wide.
- Wide desktop: around 1920 px wide.

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout. Rework grids, type sizes, image crops, navigation, and motion for touch screens.

# 9. Suggested technical direction

Use the existing repository and its conventions if a codebase already exists. Do not replace working infrastructure without a clear reason.

**Recommended starting stack for a custom-coded portfolio:**
- Next.js with the App Router.
- TypeScript.
- Tailwind CSS for layout and design tokens.
- A single primary motion library, such as GSAP or Motion, used selectively.
- MDX or structured local data for initial projects and case studies; introduce a CMS only if the team needs regular non-developer editing.
- A server-side contact form handler with validation and spam protection.
- Vercel or the existing hosting platform, once access and deployment approval are available.

The exact package versions must be selected at implementation time using current stable releases and compatibility checks. Do not blindly install outdated versions from this document.

## Engineering standards

- Responsive from the first implementation, not as a final patch.
- Semantic HTML and accessible labels.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation and interactive controls.
- Visible focus states and sufficient colour contrast.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Optimised and correctly sized images, with appropriate formats and lazy loading where useful.
- Avoid large background videos on mobile unless they are essential and optimised.
- Avoid unnecessary JavaScript, repeated animation libraries, and excessive client-side rendering.
- Correct page titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, sitemap, and robots configuration.
- Use descriptive image alt text; decorative images should have empty alt text.
- Validate forms on both client and server.
- Do not expose API keys or secrets in client-side code.
- Add an `.env.example` with placeholder variable names only. Never invent credentials.
- Include loading, empty, success, and error states where relevant.
- Add a useful 404 page.
- Test the site with keyboard navigation and on a real or emulated mobile viewport.

## Quality targets

Aim for a polished, fast, accessible website. Use Lighthouse and browser testing as diagnostics, not as a substitute for real usability testing. Avoid sacrificing legibility, accessibility, or conversion clarity for animation or benchmark scores.

# 11. AI coding agent — autonomous execution brief

The following is the operational instruction for the AI coding agent. Read this entire document before making changes.

## Mission

Design and build a distinctive, production-quality first website for 6FOLIO, an integrated creative growth studio. The website must feel like a memorable digital agency experience while remaining fast, accessible, responsive, and commercially clear.

## Execution rules

1. Inspect the current repository before changing files. Identify the framework, package manager, existing components, routes, design tokens, and current state.
2. If there is no project, scaffold a suitable Next.js + TypeScript project using current stable compatible dependencies.
3. Create a short implementation plan and then execute it. Do not stop after writing the plan.
4. Build the full responsive experience, including homepage, work index, case-study template, services, approach, about, contact, and privacy placeholder if the final policy text has not been supplied.
5. Use the six service pillars and the 6FOLIO Growth Loop defined in this document. Keep the copy editable and centralised where practical.
6. Create original layouts and interactions inspired by the references, but do not reproduce their exact layouts, copy, artwork, code, or brand identity.
7. If real team details, images, client names, metrics, testimonials, social links, or contact details are missing, use clearly labelled placeholders or self-initiated concepts. Never fabricate evidence.
8. Keep the contact form functional in the available environment. If email credentials are unavailable, provide a clear configuration path and `.env.example`; do not pretend the form sends messages.
9. Do not expose secrets in source code. Do not add paid services or external accounts that require credentials without documenting the setup.
10. Use accessible components, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion handling, and responsive behaviour.
11. Avoid heavy animation that harms performance or makes the site difficult to use. Sound must remain off by default.
12. Run the available lint, type-check, build, and tests. Fix errors before completion. If a command cannot run because an environment dependency is missing, report the exact blocker.
13. Inspect the final result at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths. Fix overflow, clipping, awkward typography, broken navigation, and layout issues.
14. Do not deploy publicly, purchase a domain, connect advertising pixels, or submit the site to awards without explicit human approval and the required credentials.
15. Keep a concise `BUILD_NOTES.md` in the project documenting completed work, assumptions, environment variables, commands, and any remaining human decisions.

## Build sequence

### Phase A — Audit and foundation
- Inspect repository and environment.
- Confirm or create the app foundation.
- Define colours, type scale, spacing, breakpoints, and reusable components.
- Set up routes, navigation, and metadata.

### Phase B — Visual system
- Implement the 6FOLIO visual direction.
- Create the hero and core interaction language.
- Build reusable project cards, service modules, CTA components, and page sections.

### Phase C — Core pages
- Homepage.
- Work index and project detail template.
- Services overview and six service detail sections/pages.
- Approach / Growth Loop.
- About / team.
- Contact form and confirmation state.
- Privacy placeholder page if approved privacy text is unavailable.

### Phase D — Responsive and interaction pass
- Mobile navigation.
- Grid and image behaviour across breakpoints.
- Motion and hover states.
- Reduced-motion behaviour.
- Keyboard and focus checks.

### Phase E — Content and polish
- Add honest placeholder content and self-initiated case-study structures where assets are absent.
- Check spelling, consistency, CTA destinations, and service descriptions.
- Optimise images and remove unused dependencies.

### Phase F — Verification
- Run lint, type-check, tests, and production build.
- Review browser output at required screen widths.
- Fix errors and high-impact visual issues.
- Write `BUILD_NOTES.md` with what is ready and what needs human input.

## Definition of done

The build is considered ready for human review when:

- All core routes work.
- The visual system is consistent and recognisably 6FOLIO.
- The six services and six-stage Growth Loop are clearly explained.
- The site is responsive at mobile, tablet, laptop, and desktop widths.
- Navigation and the project enquiry flow work in the available environment.
- No fake clients, testimonials, awards, credentials, or results appear.
- Accessibility and reduced-motion basics are implemented.
- Metadata and image optimisation are in place.
- Lint, type-check, and production build pass, or blockers are documented.
- `BUILD_NOTES.md` explains configuration and outstanding decisions.

## Final report expected from the agent

At the end, report:
1. What was built.
2. Which routes are available.
3. Which checks passed and failed.
4. Any missing environment variables or credentials.
5. Any assets or factual details the human team must provide.
6. The exact local command to run the project.
7. Whether deployment is ready for human approval.

# 12. First decisions the founding team should make

Before launch, the team should agree on these items:

- Confirm that **6FOLIO** is the final brand name and how it should be written.
- Choose one final tagline.
- Select the first two or three client segments to pursue.
- Agree which six services can be delivered in-house from day one and which require partners.
- Set minimum project budgets and retainer boundaries internally.
- Confirm who owns client communication, creative approval, and final delivery approval.

**Recommended immediate priority:** create three excellent, clearly labelled concept case studies and a strong agency website before trying to market six services at full scale. A small portfolio with a convincing process is stronger than a large portfolio full of generic claims.
